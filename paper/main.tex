\begin{abstract}
This project develops an NBA forecasting system that maintains team strength
ratings and estimates pregame win probabilities. It adapts a multifactor
Elo-style and Bradley--Terry framework developed for the 2025 Wharton High
School Data Science Competition. The proposed system combines rating
differences with home-court, rest, travel, and time-zone effects, and adjusts
ratings after completed games using schedule strength and performance
information. This draft specifies the model family, data requirements, and
chronological evaluation protocol. An initial implementation and a 151-game
historical pipeline check are available. NBA parameters have not yet been
fitted and multi-season coverage remains unverified. Pilot scores establish
execution of the pipeline; they are not a final estimate of predictive
performance. Implemented pilot conventions are distinguished from the
planned fitted model.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and provenance}
The intended outputs are a team list ordered by rating, probabilities for
upcoming games, and a permanent record of previously issued predictions and
outcomes. Ratings update after completed games. In-game win probability is
outside the present scope.

The original team report supplies the conceptual framework and feature
definitions \cite{billybeanes}. Associated competition code is reference
material rather than the canonical NBA implementation \cite{referencecode}.
The NBA adaptation will define one consistent probability function, explicit
feature units, reproducible fitting, and timestamped evaluation. Any revision
to the inherited equations will be documented with the model version.

\section{Forecast timing and notation}
For a game $g$ between teams $i$ and $j$, let $\tau_g$ denote its forecast
cutoff and let $\mathcal{F}_{\tau_g}$ contain only information available by
that time. The result is $y_g=1$ when team $i$ wins and $0$ otherwise.
The forecast is
\begin{equation}
 p_g=\Pr(y_g=1\mid\mathcal{F}_{\tau_g}).
\end{equation}
The deployed policy will record the last eligible pregame forecast, its
timestamp, data cutoff, and model version. The pilot instead fixes each day's
forecasts at 10:00 America/New\_York using completed prior slates only, then
updates after that day's games. Replay rejects a prior slate whose final-play
timestamp is at or after the next cutoff, as well as a game starting before
the cutoff. Final-play times are an availability proxy; they do not establish
when a historical provider first published corrected statistics. Historical
replays are reported separately from genuinely issued pregame forecasts.

\section{Pregame probability model}
The inherited fatigue construction is
\begin{align}
 F_{i,g}&=RD_{j,g}+\exp(|TZ_{i,g}|/3)+\nu TD_{i,g},\\
 G_{i,g}&=RD_{i,g}+\exp(|TZ_{j,g}|/3)+\nu TD_{j,g}.
\end{align}
These correspond to opponent rest/team fatigue and team rest/opponent fatigue.
This functional form is implemented for the pilot. The factor 3 specifies a time-zone
scale in hours, and $\nu$ converts the travel contribution to the numerical
scale of this index. These are empirical indices, not measurements of
physiological fatigue.

Using natural logarithms, the implemented pilot probability is
\begin{equation}
 p_{i,g}=\left[1+b^{z_{i,g}}\right]^{-1},\qquad
 z_{i,g}=\frac{r_{j,g}-r_{i,g}-Hh_{i,g}}{c}
       +\gamma\log\frac{F_{i,g}}{G_{i,g}},
 \label{eq:probability}
\end{equation}
where $b>1$, $c>0$, and the fatigue indices must be positive.
The opponent probability is computed as $1-p_{i,g}$. Full-precision
probabilities are retained; rounding is only for display.

The probability can equivalently be written
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{logit}(p_{i,g})=
 a(r_{i,g}-r_{j,g}+Hh_{i,g})-d\log(F_{i,g}/G_{i,g}),
 \quad a=\frac{\log b}{c},\quad d=\gamma\log b.
\end{equation}
This exposes redundant parameterizations. The pilot fixes $b=10$ and preserves
the original report's initial log-odds scales by setting
\begin{equation}
 c=993.39\frac{\log 10}{\log 99.39},\qquad
 \gamma=0.02\frac{\log 99.39}{\log 10}.
\end{equation}
It uses $K=32$, $H=72.52$, $\nu=10^{-6}$, $\phi=0.7$, and $\lambda=0.02$ as
unfitted starting values. A
Bayesian optimization algorithm for constants, if used, does not itself make
the rating model a Bayesian posterior model.

\section{Updates after completed games}
Let $s_{i,g}=2y_{i,g}-1$. The inherited update family is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
 r_{i,g}^{+}=r_{i,g}+K(y_{i,g}-p_{i,g})
 &(1+s_{i,g}S_{i,g})\\[-2pt]
 {}\times &(1+\phi\log_2(1+L_{i,g}))
 (1+s_{i,g}\lambda(Q_{i,g}-1)).
\end{split}
\label{eq:update}
\end{equation}
Both teams' updates use the same pregame rating state, their own performance
features, and complementary probabilities. Schedule strength uses past data;
the current result, largest lead, and box score become available only after
completion. No current-game box-score feature enters its pregame forecast.

Equation~\ref{eq:update} is implemented with the pilot conventions below;
it is not yet a fitted NBA estimator. The pilot starts ratings at 1500,
constrains $0\leq\lambda\leq0.25$, and centres the mean rating at 1500 after
each daily slate. Asymmetric updates need not conserve the sum of ratings;
centring preserves pairwise differences. Each season is replayed independently.
Inter-season carryover remains a future modeling choice. Duplicate game IDs
are rejected, and both team updates are committed only after both calculations
succeed.

\subsection{Schedule strength}
The original feature has the form
\begin{equation}
 S_{i,g}=\frac{2W_{j,g}+\overline{W}_{\mathcal{O}(j),g}}{3},
\end{equation}
where $W_{j,g}$ is the opponent's pregame win fraction and
$\overline{W}_{\mathcal{O}(j),g}$ summarizes that opponent's prior opponents.
The pilot uses Laplace-smoothed win fractions $(w+1)/(n+2)$ and weights repeated
opponents by their frequency in the opponent's prior schedule. An empty
opponent history receives 0.5. History resets at the season boundary.
Full-season win fractions cannot be substituted into an earlier game's features.

\subsection{Lead retention and performance}
The implemented pilot lead-retention ratio is
\begin{equation}
 L_{i,g}=\frac{|PTS_{i,g}-PTS_{j,g}|}{\max(LL_{i,g},LL_{j,g})},
\end{equation}
where $LL$ denotes the team's largest lead. Using the peak absolute lead
extends the inherited denominator to teams that never led, with $0<L\leq1$
for a non-tied completed game. This is a deliberate pilot revision: it measures
final margin relative to the game's peak advantage for either side. Source
records are rejected if the peak lead is smaller than the final margin.

For eight box-score features $X_{k,i,g}$, the pilot scales are
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde{\boldsymbol X}=(X_1/10,X_2,X_3,X_4,X_5,X_6/3,-X_7,X_8).
\end{equation}
The negative sign on the turnover fraction encodes poorer performance from
more turnovers. These fixed numerical scales have not been optimized.
With $\boldsymbol\alpha=(0.25,0.20,0.15,0.10,0.10,0.10,0.05,0.05)$,
\begin{align}
 U_{i,g}&=\sum_{k=1}^{8}\alpha_k\widetilde X_{k,i,g},\\
 A_{i,g}&=\operatorname{clip}\!\left((1+e^{-U_{i,g}})^{-1},10^{-6},1-10^{-6}\right),\\
 Q_{i,g}&=\operatorname{clip}\!\left(A_{i,g}/\overline A_{i,<g},0.25,4\right).
\end{align}
The reference history is the mean of eligible prior games, excluding the
current game. An opening game receives $Q=1$. The logistic transformation
replaces the inherited $U/(1+U)$ singularity at $U=-1$ while retaining signed
net efficiency. These changes require validation before adoption in the
final fitted model.

\section{Data construction and quality control}
The implemented collection uses ESPN's publicly accessible scoreboard and
game-summary responses for game IDs, teams, tip-off times, venues, statuses,
final scores, box scores, and largest leads \cite{espn}. These endpoints have
no documented availability guarantee. GeoNames city coordinates and IANA
time zones supply a city-centre travel proxy \cite{geonames}. Exact arena
coordinates and multi-season source coverage remain future work.
NBA endpoints through \texttt{nba\_api} remain an alternative candidate
\cite{nbaapi}. Data acquisition must use free access.

Canonical tables contain one record per game and one record per team-game.
Count and fraction fields receive distinct names. Validation checks include
unique keys, two opponents per completed game, score agreement, field types,
and missing-value flags. Genuine blowouts are retained. Source responses,
retrieval timestamps, and hashes support reproducibility and correction handling.
For missing largest lead, a play-by-play score path may supply the value only
when its final scores agree with the header. If team shooting totals disagree
with the final score, complete player rows may replace the aggregate only
when their point sum, shooting sum, and the final score path agree. Non-player
turnovers are then derived from participant-free turnover events. Each repair
is recorded in the game data; unresolved records prevent archive publication.

Rest is the number of intervening dates in America/New\_York, making a
back-to-back zero. The opening-game prior is three rest days, zero travel,
and zero time-zone change, flagged as an initialization convention.
Travel is estimated between consecutive venue cities, not the actual team
itinerary. Time-zone differences compare both locations' UTC offsets at the
current tip-off instant, so a daylight-saving transition alone does not count
as geographic travel. Unknown or ambiguous cities cause validation failure.

\section{Parameter fitting and evaluation protocol}
For parameters $\theta$, replay the training games in chronological order,
predicting before updating. A proposed objective is mean binary log loss:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{L}(\theta)=-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{g=1}^{N}
 \left[y_g\log p_g(\theta)+(1-y_g)\log(1-p_g(\theta))\right].
\end{equation}
Every candidate parameter set requires its own rating replay. Numerical
probability clipping, if needed, is fixed and disclosed. Feature scaling,
imputation, constraints, and any regularization are learned or selected
without access to the final test outcomes.

Subject to data coverage, the proposed split is 2021--22 through 2023--24 for
training, 2024--25 for validation, and 2025--26 for final testing. Parameters
and formula choices are frozen before test evaluation. Ratings may update
after each observed test result, matching the intended live process.

Probability evaluation includes log loss, the Brier score
\begin{equation}
 BS=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{g=1}^{N}(p_g-y_g)^2,
\end{equation}
and calibration plots comparing forecast probabilities with observed win
fractions. Report bin counts and uncertainty alongside calibration estimates.
Winner accuracy is secondary, with a stated tie rule for $p=0.5$. Evaluation
must disclose sample counts, excluded games, and the forecast cutoff policy.

\section{Current evidence and pending results}
As a reference check, the original prediction function reproduced all ten
rounded home-team probabilities in the competition report using the repository's
supplied matchup ratings. This verifies that example's probability outputs;
it does not reproduce the historical rating path or establish forecast accuracy.

The first pipeline check covers October 22 through November 10, 2024: all
151 discovered regular-season games were parsed, involving all 30 teams.
One game's largest leads were recovered from play-by-play; another game's
two team aggregates were recovered from player rows. Earlier games initialize
the rating state. The 58 games from November 4 through November 10 yield
the following diagnostic scores with unfitted starting parameters:

\midrule
Evaluation games & 58 \\
Mean log loss & 0.6110 \\
Brier score & 0.2104 \\

These figures are a short-sample execution check, not final predictive

issued live forecasts remain pending.

\section{Deployment and reproducibility}
A scheduled Python process will ingest completed games, validate features,
update rating state, and save forecasts. Streamlit will display those outputs
without refitting on page visits. Failed ingestion preserves the previous
valid snapshot and exposes its age. Corrected results may require replaying
rating state but must not overwrite previously issued forecasts.
The implemented interface includes a season selector shared across Teams,
Games, and Methodology. It defaults to the current season, including a pending
or initialization view before forecasts are available. Processed historical
game records and forecast snapshots are stored by season in GitHub; raw
provider responses remain outside Git history. Partial season coverage and
historical replay status are displayed explicitly. All pilot seasons start
independently; archival support does not imply fitted inter-season carryover.

Each release will identify the source-code revision, model configuration,
data snapshot or manifest, preprocessing rules, random seed, and evaluation
interval. This manuscript will be revised alongside material changes to
features, equations, fitting, or evaluation. The repository remains private
during development; manuscript draft status does not imply deployment.

\appendix
\section{Box-score feature derivations}
Let $O$ denote the opponent, with all quantities belonging to one completed
game. Percentages are represented as fractions unless explicitly stated.
Possessions are estimated as
\begin{equation}
 \widehat{P}=FGA+0.44FTA-OREB+TOV.
\end{equation}
The following definitions form the implemented pilot feature contract:
\begin{align}
 X_1&=100(PTS-PTS_O)/\widehat{P},\\
 X_2&=(FGM+0.5\,3PM)/FGA,\\
 X_3&=PTS/[2(FGA+0.44FTA)],\\
 X_4&=OREB/(OREB+DREB_O),\\
 X_5&=DREB/(DREB+OREB_O),\\
 X_6&=AST/\max(TOV,1),\\
 X_7&=TOV/(FGA+0.44FTA+TOV),\\
 X_8&=STL/\widehat{P}_O.
\end{align}
$X_1$ retains the inherited shared-possession approximation for net
efficiency. Possession estimation conventions must be consistent across
teams and seasons. $X_4$ and $X_5$ explicitly use the opposite rebound
category from the opponent. $X_8$ proposes an opponent-possession denominator,
revising the report's expression that omitted offensive rebounds. These
choices are implemented for the pilot and require validation before final
fitting. Zero turnovers use one in the assist-ratio denominator; other
nonpositive denominators cause validation failure.

\section{Travel derivation}
For venue latitude $\varphi$ and longitude $\ell$ in radians, estimated
great-circle distance is
\begin{align}
 a_v&=\sin^2\!\left(\frac{\varphi_2-\varphi_1}{2}\right)
 +\cos\varphi_1\cos\varphi_2
 \sin^2\!\left(\frac{\ell_2-\ell_1}{2}\right),\\
 TD&=2R\arcsin\sqrt{a_v},\qquad R=6371\ \text{km}.
\end{align}
The computation clips $a_v$ to $[0,1]$ for floating-point safety. The pilot
uses the actual game venue's city, including neutral venues, rather than
always substituting the franchise's home city. Exact arena coordinates remain
an intended improvement.
