\begin{abstract}
An NBA forecasting system maintains team ratings and estimates pregame win
probabilities using a dynamic multifactor rating model. Rating differences, home court, rest, estimated travel, and time-zone
changes determine pregame probabilities. Outcomes, schedule strength, lead
retention, and box-score performance determine subsequent rating updates.
Model version 0.2.0 is fitted on 2021--22 through 2023--24 regular-season
history and frozen before evaluation on 2024--25 and 2025--26. Five validated
full seasons contain 6,150 games. On 1,075 eligible games in the held-out
2025--26 season, log loss is 0.5925, Brier score is 0.2035, and winner accuracy
is 68.56\%. These are reconstructed historical forecasts, not predictions
issued live. The manuscript specifies feature derivations, bounded parameter
estimation, data repairs, and the limitations of this first NBA calibration.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope}
The system produces teams ordered by rating, matchup probabilities, and a
record of forecasts and outcomes. Live ratings update after
completed games; in-game probability is outside scope. The model uses explicit feature
units, stable transformations, one complementary probability function, and
NBA-specific calibration. The following sections specify the equations,
data construction, fitting protocol, and evaluation.
The website, historical replay, and manually invoked live collector are
implemented. Automatic collection is scheduled through GitHub Actions; private frontend
hosting is deployed with private access.

\section{Forecast timing}
Let $\tau_g$ be a forecast cutoff and $\mathcal F_{\tau_g}$ the information
available by then. A home-team forecast estimates
$p_g=\Pr(y_g=1\mid\mathcal F_{\tau_g})$, where $y_g$ is the home-win indicator.
The historical replay freezes all forecasts for an Eastern-calendar daily
slate at the earlier of 10:00 America/New\_York and one hour before its first
tip-off. It requires all prior slates to have completed before that cutoff;
current-slate results cannot enter its forecasts. Ratings update after the
slate. A team cannot appear twice on one slate, and duplicate games are rejected.

Completion availability is proxied by terminal EndGame wallclock timestamps,
rather than later edited play timestamps. Seventeen games use dated final-recap
publication times. One game with source play dates shifted by a day uses an
explicitly flagged conservative tip-off-plus-eight-hours availability bound;
this is not an observed completion time. Replays check these proxies against
the next cutoff but cannot establish when historical corrections first reached
a live feed. Genuinely issued forecasts record their actual issue time,
data cutoff, and model version in a separately preserved ledger.

\section{Pregame probability}
For home team $i$ and away team $j$, define pregame ratings $r_i,r_j$, rest
$RD$ in days, travel $TD$ in kilometres, and time-zone change $TZ$ in hours.
Let $h=1$ at a home venue and $h=0$ at a neutral venue. The fatigue
indices, with an empirically fitted travel coefficient $\nu$, are
\begin{align}
 F_i&=RD_j+\exp(|TZ_i|/3)+\nu TD_i,\\
 G_i&=RD_i+\exp(|TZ_j|/3)+\nu TD_j.
\end{align}
They are empirical indices, not physiological measurements. The probability is
\begin{equation}
 p_i=\left[1+10^{z_i}\right]^{-1},\qquad
 z_i=\frac{r_j-r_i-Hh}{c}+\gamma\ln(F_i/G_i),\qquad p_j=1-p_i.
 \label{eq:probability}
\end{equation}
All logarithms are natural unless explicitly subscripted. Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{logit}(p_i)=\ln(10)
 \left[\frac{r_i-r_j+Hh}{c}-\gamma\ln(F_i/G_i)\right].
\end{equation}
The exponent base and rating scale cannot both be freely identified. Moreover,
rescaling ratings, $K$, $H$, and $c$ together preserves probabilities. We fix
the base at 10 and $c=993.39\ln(10)/\ln(99.39)=497.3558141396255$ to anchor
the rating scale, then fit $K$, $H$, and $\gamma$. Display rounding
does not affect calculations.

\section{Rating updates}
For either team, let $y$ denote its own win indicator, $p$ its complementary
pregame probability, and $s=2y-1$. The implemented update is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
 r^+=r+K(y-p)(1+s\beta S)
 (1+\phi\log_2(1+L))
 (1+s\lambda(Q-1)).
\end{split}
\label{eq:update}
\end{equation}
Here $\beta$ is a fitted schedule-strength multiplier, $\phi$ weights lead
retention, and $\lambda$ weights relative performance. Both teams use their
pregame ratings and their own performance history. The result and all
current-game box-score features enter only after completion. Each season
starts independently at 1500. Asymmetric updates can shift the league mean;
recentering it at 1500 after each slate preserves all pairwise differences.
Inter-season carryover is not part of this release.

\subsection{Schedule strength}
For team $i$ facing $j$, define
\begin{equation}
 S_i=\frac{2W_j+\overline W_{\mathcal O(j)}}{3},\qquad
 W_t=\frac{w_t+1}{n_t+2}.
\end{equation}
All wins $w_t$ and games $n_t$ precede the cutoff. The second term averages
smoothed win rates of $j$'s previous opponents, including repeated opponents
in proportion to their frequency. Empty opponent history receives 0.5.
This schedule-strength feature uses only results available before the cutoff.

\subsection{Lead retention and relative performance}
For largest leads $LL_i,LL_j$ and final scores $PTS_i,PTS_j$, use
\begin{equation}
 L=\frac{|PTS_i-PTS_j|}{\max(LL_i,LL_j)}.
\end{equation}
Using the larger of both peak leads defines the ratio even for teams that never led.
Valid non-tied completed games satisfy $0<L\leq1$; peak leads below the final
margin are rejected. This measures final margin relative to either side's
peak advantage, not a separate losing-team retention ratio.

For the eight Appendix~\ref{app:features} features, fixed scales are
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde{\boldsymbol X}=(X_1/10,X_2,X_3,X_4,X_5,X_6/3,-X_7,X_8).
\end{equation}
The turnover sign rewards fewer turnovers. Positive weights sum to one:
\begin{align}
 U&=\sum_{k=1}^8\alpha_k\widetilde X_k,\\
 A&=\operatorname{clip}\left((1+e^{-U})^{-1},10^{-6},1-10^{-6}\right),\\
 Q&=\operatorname{clip}\left(A/\overline A_{<g},0.25,4\right).
\end{align}
$A$ is the aggregate game performance factor (AGPF), and $Q$ is the performance
adjustment factor (PAF). The reference is the expanding mean of that team's
prior games only. Openers use $Q=1$. The sigmoid maps the aggregate to a bounded positive value
while preserving signed net efficiency in its input. With
$0\leq\lambda\leq0.25$ and the stated PAF range, the performance multiplier
cannot become negative. These transformations and scales were fixed before
fitting; the eight weights were fitted.

\section{Data construction}
Free ESPN-derived schedule, team-box-score, and play-by-play parquet files from
SportsDataverse provide the initial history \cite{sportsdataverse}. Primary
ESPN game summaries replace incomplete or inconsistent archival records
\cite{espn}. GeoNames supplies venue-city coordinates and IANA time zones
\cite{geonames}. Each of the five seasons contains 1,230 completed standard
regular-season games, 30 teams, and 82 games per team. Preseason, playoffs,
All-Star games, and the NBA Cup final are excluded; Cup games that count
toward the regular season remain included.

Validation requires unique game/team identities, matching opponent rows and
header scores, finite nonnegative integer counts, made shots no greater than
attempts, scoring identities, plausible assists and steals, valid feature
denominators, and peak leads consistent with final margins. Missing values
are not converted to zero. Largest leads can be recovered from a score path
only when its final scores match the header. Inconsistent team aggregates
can be replaced by complete player rows with matching points and shooting
identities. Turnovers consistently mean summed player turnovers, excluding
provider non-player team turnovers. Player and provider non-player team turnovers are kept distinct.

All required numerical formula inputs are complete. Repair actions, availability

a daylight-saving change alone from creating geographic travel. Ambiguous
or unknown cities cause validation failure.

\section{Parameter estimation}
Training uses 2021--22 through 2023--24; 2024--25 is validation and 2025--26 is
a held-out test. The objective is mean binary log loss,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L(\theta)=-\frac1N\sum_g
 [y_g\ln p_g(\theta)+(1-y_g)\ln(1-p_g(\theta))].
\end{equation}
A game is scored after both teams have played ten prior games, giving 3,220
training observations. Earlier games still initialize histories, update
ratings, and retain forecasts. Every candidate recomputes parameter-dependent
ratings and AGPF history. An accelerated Numba replay is checked against the
canonical Python engine before fitting and at the fitted solution, to
probability tolerance $10^{-11}$.

SciPy differential evolution with L-BFGS-B polishing \cite{scipy} fits 14 free
dimensions: seven scalar coefficients and seven softmax logits. With the eighth
logit fixed to zero, $\alpha_k=\exp(u_k)/\sum_l\exp(u_l)$ and $u_8=0$.
Travel is optimized on a base-10 logarithmic scale. Settings are random seed
42, population multiplier 8, maximum 150 generations, relative tolerance
$10^{-5}$, one worker, and polishing enabled. The run converged in 44 generations
and 5,520 objective evaluations. Training log loss decreased from 0.637588 at
the reference starting configuration to 0.628210. Constants were frozen at
03:04:25 UTC on October 13, 2026 (October 12 Eastern), before validation/test
evaluation. No parameters or formula choices were changed after those scores.

\section{Historical evaluation}
Probability quality is assessed by log loss, Brier score
$N^{-1}\sum_g(p_g-y_g)^2$, and ten equal-width calibration bins with counts,
mean forecasts, and observed home-win fractions. Lower log loss and Brier
score are better. Winner accuracy is secondary, choosing home at exactly
$p=0.5$. It is not an accuracy percentage for probabilities.

\section{Simple Elo comparison and chronological diagnostics}
A comparator estimates only $K$ and $H$, with the same fixed $c$, season
initialization, cutoff, and warmup as the full model. Its equations are
\begin{equation}
 p_i=[1+10^{(r_j-r_i-Hh)/c}]^{-1},\qquad
 r_i^+=r_i+K(y_i-p_i),\quad r_j^+=r_j-K(y_i-p_i).
\end{equation}
Fatigue, schedule, lead, and performance coefficients are zero. Both models
fit training log loss with identical bounds on $K$ and $H$, seed 42, and the
same differential-evolution and polishing settings. Comparator predictions
are checked against the canonical engine. Data and scored games are paired.
This diagnostic was added after viewing original results; it is not a new
untouched test or prospective evaluation.

Paired exploratory uncertainty estimates use 10,000 bootstrap resamples of
whole Eastern-calendar daily slates. Each sampled slate retains its eligible
games, and differences are weighted by the total sampled game count. This
preserves within-slate dependence but does not model dependence across days

descriptive evidence rather than a guarantee of future improvement.

to 47.570, and $\gamma$ from 0.1647 to 0.1829. Individual travel and performance
weights are less stable: $\nu$ ranges from $1.32\times10^{-7}$ to
$1.10\times10^{-4}$, and the defensive-rebound weight $\alpha_5$ ranges from
0.4795 to 0.9610. Lead and performance coefficients remain at their upper
bounds in every fit. The checks support a modest repeated probability-score
advantage for the model family, while leaving uncertainty about unique feature
effects and overfitting. Attributing improvement to individual features would
require an ablation study.

The original v0.2 configuration is retained provisionally for live integration.
The four-season refit is not promoted merely because its now-observed score
is slightly better. Source hashes, per-fold fit reports, metrics, paired
predictions, and intervals are saved under the comparison release directory.
Future issued forecasts will provide prospective monitoring. The historical
outcomes were already available during development, so these expanding-window
checks must not be presented as newly untouched evidence.

\section{Reproducibility and application}
The repository stores clean game records, two-row-per-game box-score tables,
derived-feature tables, and reconstructed forecasts under season directories.
Pregame and postgame feature columns are explicitly distinguished. Model
configuration, optimizer bounds, seed, convergence trace, coverage audits,
and source manifests identify the release. Large raw parquet downloads and
ESPN response caches stay outside Git. The documented scripts reproduce
acquisition, validation, fitting, and export; provider archives may change,
so hashes identify the exact source snapshots used here.

Streamlit displays Teams, Games, and Methodology for a shared selected season,
defaulting to the current season. Archived seasons expose scores and downloadable
CSV files. The first observed 2026--27 snapshot holds initial ratings and three issued
upcoming regular-season forecasts. No regular-season results have arrived in
that snapshot; preseason outcomes are excluded. Light/dark appearance modes and fixed dropdowns are supported.
The manual collector validates completed games, applies the frozen model,
and saves upcoming forecasts without fitting during page interactions.
GitHub Actions schedules collection every two hours.
Failed ingestion must preserve the last valid output and expose freshness.
Corrected records may trigger rating replay but cannot overwrite genuinely
issued forecasts. The repository remains private during development.

\section{Observed after-game pipeline}
The current-season collector refreshes individual ESPN scoreboard dates and
validated final summaries. Provider whitelist calendar metadata restricts
requests to known season dates; the default checks a seven-day lookback,
unresolved events, and seven Eastern-calendar dates ahead. A full-refresh
option revisits the season calendar for older corrections. Unknown regular
categories, missing known finals, incomplete box scores, or inconsistent
identities block publication. Preseason, All-Star, and Cup-final events remain
excluded. A single-writer filesystem lock and atomic bundle replacement
protect concurrent updates and preserve a valid snapshot on ingestion failure.
The application shows last-successful freshness and failed-attempt status.

Rating-state reconstruction and forecast issuance are separate. An internal
timeline predicts the rating-update expectation at each tip-off from earlier
source-completion events, then updates and recenters at individual completions.
These estimates enable backfill and correction replay, but are not treated as
genuinely issued predictions. Completion proxies do not establish when a live
provider first made every historical statistic available.

Observed runs issue probabilities only for scheduled games still in the future,
using an actual timestamp after source retrieval; the CLI cannot backdate.
A change in rating state, configuration, or announced context creates a new
revision. Identical inputs reuse the previous revision. Current ratings are
held fixed while announced intervening schedules project future rest and venue
travel; unknown future results are never fabricated. Revisions retain their
probabilities, timestamps, input hashes, pregame context, and configuration
registry references. Correction replay changes current state and future
forecasts without rewriting earlier issued probabilities.

For a completed game, evaluation selects the latest stored revision issued
before its recorded start with matching opponents. A game discovered only
after completion receives no issued probability and is excluded from live
forecast metrics, even though it updates ratings. Primary live scoring requires
both teams to have ten observed prior games at issuance; all-issued descriptive
scores are stored separately. Evaluation joins forecasts to the latest
validated outcomes.

This live policy updates after individual completions when the collector runs,
whereas the historical evaluation froze daily slates. Consequently, the
historical accuracy is not a measured accuracy of the live service. The first
three forecasts were saved on October 13, 2026 Eastern; prospective results
remain pending. Tests cover repeated polling, corrected outcomes, preservation
of revisions, missed-game backfill, invalid future timestamps, failed
collection, writer locking, and empty/issued/backfilled interface states.
Source connectivity has been verified and automatic collection is scheduled.
Private frontend hosting is deployed and verified.

\section{Scheduled operation and hosting status}
The GitHub Actions workflow invokes core checks and collection at minute 17
of every two UTC hours. Scheduled jobs have a three-minute timeout; manually
requested full-refresh jobs allow ten minutes. Official checkout and Python
setup actions are pinned to verified commit hashes. No source cache or workflow
artifact is uploaded. The collector commits processed season data and status
only, and refuses publication if the branch advanced during collection. An
ingestion failure can publish failure status while retaining valid ratings.
Scheduling is best-effort rather than an immediate final-score push service.
The first hosted Ubuntu 24.04 run passed 36 core checks (nine optional
frontend/fitting checks skipped), refreshed sources, and committed a valid
snapshot in 40 seconds. The complete local suite passes all 45 tests.
The runner platform is pinned to the verified Ubuntu release.

The workflow uses included private-repository allowances; the account's existing
zero-dollar Actions budget blocks paid usage. Allowances are shared with other
repositories, so exhaustion may interrupt updates. No paid service, billing
limit increase, or new credentials are configured. Cross-platform verification
requires identical record structures and identities, with floating-point
agreement within relative/absolute tolerance $10^{-12}$; this permits platform
rounding without changing the model or its stored forecasts.

The Streamlit application is deployed from the private repository's main
branch, root application, and tested Python 3.12 runtime at
\url{https://eshan-nba-elo-forecast.streamlit.app/}.
Private sharing was verified on the deployed application and the GitHub
repository remains private. Hosted checks verified current-season defaults,
archived season switching, held-out metrics, downloadable CSV data, and
light/dark appearance modes. The downloaded 2025--26 box-score table contains
2,460 rows matching every committed field; line-ending normalization does not
alter the records.

An active application refreshes its saved snapshot every minute using a
Streamlit fragment. This does not scrape or fit during page interactions;
backend collection remains on its separate two-hour schedule. Snapshot
freshness is visible, and actual future issued forecasts will establish
prospective performance. Deployment verification does not imply that current
season games have already produced a live accuracy estimate.

\section{Box-score feature derivations}\label{app:features}
All quantities below refer to one completed team-game; $O$ denotes its opponent.
Counts retain count units; rate features are fractions. Using player turnovers,
\begin{equation}
 \widehat P=FGA+0.44FTA-OREB+TOV.
\end{equation}
The eight derived features are
\begin{align}
 X_1&=100(PTS-PTS_O)/\widehat P,\\
 X_2&=(FGM+0.5\,3PM)/FGA,\\
 X_3&=PTS/[2(FGA+0.44FTA)],\\
 X_4&=OREB/(OREB+DREB_O),\\
 X_5&=DREB/(DREB+OREB_O),\\
 X_6&=AST/\max(TOV,1),\\
 X_7&=TOV/(FGA+0.44FTA+TOV),\\
 X_8&=STL/\widehat P_O.
\end{align}
$X_1$ uses a shared-possession net-efficiency approximation.
$X_2$ credits three-point makes with half an additional made shot; $X_3$ scales
points by estimated shooting opportunities. Offensive and defensive rebound
fractions use opposite opponent rebound categories. $X_6$ uses one for zero turnovers. $X_7$ is a
turnover fraction. $X_8$ uses opponent estimated possessions, including the
offensive-rebound subtraction. Other
nonpositive denominators fail validation. All these features affect subsequent
ratings, never the same game's pregame probability.

\section{Travel and time-zone derivation}
For venue latitude $\varphi$ and longitude $\ell$ in radians,
\begin{align}
 a_v&=\sin^2((\varphi_2-\varphi_1)/2)
 +\cos\varphi_1\cos\varphi_2\sin^2((\ell_2-\ell_1)/2),\\
 TD&=2R\arcsin\sqrt{\operatorname{clip}(a_v,0,1)},\qquad R=6371\ \text{km}.
\end{align}
Neutral-game venue cities are used rather than substituting a franchise home
city. If $o(v,t)$ is a venue's dated UTC offset in hours, then
$TZ=o(v_{\mathrm{current}},t_g)-o(v_{\mathrm{previous}},t_g)$.
Exact arenas and actual itineraries are not measured by these proxies.
